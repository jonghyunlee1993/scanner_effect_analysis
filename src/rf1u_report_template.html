What a scanner does to a foundation model,
and how far images can undo it

A 109-slide cohort in which every slide was imaged by all six scanners at the same physical locations. That pairing lets us separate what the scanner changes from what the tissue changes, bound what any correction could achieve, and test corrections against a fidelity constraint rather than against scanner separability alone. The argument runs in three steps: after colour is matched the residual is spatial frequency; correcting it is nearly free and does work; and even so it caps out well short of what a correction in feature space reaches.

1 · How to read this

The six quantities everything below is measured in

Every number in this report is one of six measurements. They are defined here once, with the formula and the plain-language reading, because several of them are easy to confuse and two of them are degenerate if read alone.

Band power
image domain

Convert the patch to optical density, OD = −log(RGB), and take the mean across the three channels. Blur that with Gaussians of σ = 1, 2 and 4 pixels and subtract successive blurs. What remains are three detail bands — fine, mid and coarse structure — plus a low-pass base that carries the colour:

b₁ = m − mσ1 · b₂ = mσ1 − mσ2 · b₃ = mσ2 − mσ4 · base = mσ4

Band power is the mean square of one band over the pixels of a patch, Ek = mean(bk²). It is a variance in a spatial frequency band: how much structure the image carries at that scale. At 0.5052 µm per pixel, σ = 1 px is roughly cellular texture and σ = 4 px is roughly nuclear and glandular outline. “Fine band power” means E₁.

ERT
image domain

Effective relative transfer. For the same tissue imaged by two scanners, the ratio of radially averaged power spectral density in a defined frequency band. Reported against AT2, so AT2 is 1.000 by construction and GT450 at 1.478 transmits about half again as much high-frequency energy.

We deliberately do not call this an MTF. It is measured on tissue, not on a calibration target, so optics, sharpening, sampling and JPEG all fold into it. It is an observation about the acquisition chain, not a device specification.

Scanner radius
representation

Take one physical location, take the six scanners' embeddings of it, L2-normalize them, and measure the RMS distance from the six points to their own centroid. Average over the 100 locations of a slide.

It answers: for the same spot, how far apart do the six acquisitions land? Content is removed by re-centring at every location, so what remains is scanner disagreement. Raw values here run 0.212 to 0.483 on a scale whose maximum is 2. Relative radius reduction (RR) is 1 − corrected / raw.

Its minimum is degenerate. A model that mapped every patch to the same vector would score zero. That single fact is why this report never ranks a method on radius without a fidelity gate attached.

Content margin
representation

For a corrected patch, the cosine to the reference acquisition of the same physical location, minus the 95th percentile of cosines that reference has to unmatched locations on the same slide. Positive means the corrected patch still resembles its own spot more than it resembles a random other spot.

The frozen non-inferiority margin is −0.02: a correction may lose at most that much against raw before it is called unsafe.

Collapse guardrail
representation

Three geometry statistics computed per source scanner and expressed as a ratio to raw: total variance, entropy effective rank, and median pairwise distance. A correction that quietly compresses the embedding cloud shows up here even when the content margin does not.

Thresholds are 0.90 on the point estimate and 0.85 on the CI lower bound, and every source scanner must pass — not the average.

Scanner probe
representation · secondary

Fit a linear classifier on training slides to predict which of the six scanners produced an embedding, and score balanced accuracy on slides it never saw. Chance is {{PROBE_CHANCE}}.

Also degenerate alone, and for the same reason: a constant representation reaches chance. It is reported here beside content and collapse, never instead of them. It answers a different question from radius — not “how far apart are the clusters” but “does any direction still separate them” — and section 9 shows the two can disagree sharply.

All inference is blocked by physical slide. Bootstraps resample slides, not patches, with 5,000 replicates and seed 20260803; cross-validation splits slides. Patches within a slide are observations, not independent replicates, and are never treated as such.
2 · The cohort

Why this dataset can answer the question

109 physical slides spanning 37 tissue types, each scanned on all six instruments, with 100 tissue locations per slide-scanner brought onto a common physical grid at 0.5052 µm/px. Every comparison in this report is therefore between six images of the same cells, which is what makes it possible to attribute a difference to the instrument rather than to the biology.

Establishing that grid was most of the work. 654 native WSI headers were audited and all 654 opened with consistent MPP; the final geometry passed 512-pixel bounds and identity checks on 65,400 of 65,400 scanner-locations and 10,900 of 10,900 six-scanner tuples. Because resampling a scan can itself change its high-frequency content, the render chain uses explicit anti-aliasing and was audited against a pre-specified alias limit of 5%: the worst observed alias-to-in-band ratio was 0.0154 for sinusoids and 0.0190 for broadband noise.

The tissue labels are normal tissue types only. There is no independent biological or clinical outcome in this cohort, so nothing here is called biological fidelity or clinical validation; the fidelity endpoints are representation-level and the tissue-type analyses are labelled coarse secondary evidence throughout.

So the scanner effect is a scanner × content response, not a detachable global style — and the interaction differs by instrument. AKOYA and S60 respond to what tissue is on the slide; VERSA and S360 vary slide to slide in a way tissue type does not explain. Tissue classes hold one to six slides, mostly three, so this is coarse content evidence and several tissue slopes are heavily shrunk.

The same structure reappears downstream. In the correction-heterogeneity analysis, 240 REML fits found tissue-level variance in the radius benefit for 18 of 20 method-model cells and in scanner-specific content change for 71 of 100, with AKOYA heterogeneous in all 20 cells. A pooled endpoint can conceal structured per-scanner and per-tissue response, which is why the frozen safety rule requires every source scanner to pass rather than the average.

A one-parameter blur–sharpen family was also pre-specified and projected onto each scanner's curve with leave-one-slide-out cross-fitting. The residual against a matched scalar null had a 95% CI lower bound above zero for all five scanners (0.081–0.116 GT450, 0.095–0.107 VERSA, 0.256–0.316 AKOYA, 0.093–0.111 S60, 0.093–0.107 S360), so a single sharpness dial does not reproduce the effect. That is why the correction in section 7 uses three bands rather than one gain. GT450 also hit the family's gain = 2 boundary in 90.8% of folds, so part of its residual reflects range rather than dimensionality.
4 · White space

What the glass tells us, and what it does not

Empty background is the obvious place to characterize a scanner: no biology, just the instrument. We extracted 65,400 raw white-space patches, one set per slide-scanner, and asked whether background noise and optical-density features add explanatory power to the within-slide tissue transfer already modelled from tissue itself.

The test compares two nested models per scanner and frequency band, y ~ tissue-side predictors against y ~ tissue-side predictors + background NPS + background OD, on the within-slide AT2 contrast. It fails on every criterion.

5 · Bounds

Low scanner radius can mean alignment or destruction

Before benchmarking real corrections we bounded the region with physical controls — interventions that are not deployable but whose effect on the representation is known by construction. 2,354,400 control embeddings, four models, slide-blocked inference.

This is the study's central negative result

Deleting high-frequency content cuts scanner radius by up to half — a bigger reduction than any real method achieves — while destroying the representation. The paired oracle improves radius by a third as much and keeps content intact. Radius alone cannot tell these two apart. Everything downstream is therefore judged on radius, content and collapse jointly, and this frontier is what a real correction is measured against.

The oracle uses the registered same-location acquisition of another scanner, so it is not deployable. Its role is to show that a fidelity-preserving invariance gain exists at all, and to give the achievable region a ceiling.

The Fit needs column matters as much as the numbers. Two of the five require registered same-location data to fit, and one of those is the best method in the table: orthogonal Procrustes aligns paired embeddings through the SVD of X'Y. The strongest deployable-without-pairing option is CORAL, at +16.3% to +34.9%, and that is the realistic feature-space recommendation for a site that cannot rescan its slides on a reference instrument.

Three further observations carry forward, and the first is the hinge of this report. Frequency calibration applied to raw images does essentially nothing: +0.5%, −0.1%, −0.5% and +0.3% across the four models. The same physical quantity corrected after colour matching is worth +2.8 to +5.0 points (section 9). The spectrum is not a weak lever — it is a lever that only engages once the colour offset is out of the way, which is why nobody looking at raw-image frequency correction would have found it. Second, paired OD affine is the cautionary case: it improves UNI's radius by 8.9% with a positive CI and is still ruled unsafe, because VERSA's variance-trace ratio lands at 0.8865 against a 0.90 threshold. An invariance gain that fails fidelity is not a marginal call under this rule. Third, the feature-space methods win on every model, and by the largest margin exactly where image methods do least.

Exact leave-one-tissue-out refitting changed none of the 20 safe or safe-and-improved decisions, so these verdicts are not an artefact of which tissues happened to be in the training folds. In the coarse tissue-type probe, held-out raw top-1 accuracy was 29.2% for ResNet50 and 50–56% for the pathology models, and correction-associated changes were mostly within noise; Procrustes was the only method to improve paired centroid-profile agreement in all four models, and no correction improved every tissue metric.

7 · The bridge

Correcting the residual, as cheaply as possible

What follows is not offered as a new normalizer competing for adoption. Its purpose is evidentiary: if spatial frequency is the residual after colour, then correcting it should pay — and if even a well-optimized version of that correction runs out of room, the ceiling is a property of the image domain rather than of one implementation. The method is deliberately the cheapest thing that can carry that argument.

Reinhard
baseline

Match the per-channel mean and standard deviation of CIE L*a*b* between the source population and the target population. No paired images needed. Safe and invariance-improving in all four models, and the strongest image method in the locked benchmark — but it cannot touch sharpness.

Ours
RF1U

The same deployment contract — population statistics only — with a per-band sharpness correction added.

  1. Split the OD mean into the three detail bands of section 1 and the colour base.
  2. For each band take the ratio of pooled band power between target and source populations, g = sqrt(E_target / E_source). This is Reinhard's moment matching applied per spatial frequency.
  3. Bootstrap the slides for that ratio's own standard error and shrink toward identity by α = max(0, 1 − 2·SE/|log g|).
  4. Add the result to all three OD channels as one shared scalar, preserving hue.
  5. Project anything out of range onto the exact RGB8 boundary rather than clipping channels independently.

Step 3 is the safety mechanism: a band whose gain cannot be estimated reliably gets α = 0, which is exact identity, and the method degrades to plain Reinhard.

coststhree numbers per source scanner on top of Reinhard's six Lab moments; one extra Gaussian pyramid at inference
needsexactly what Reinhard needs — a source population and a target population, no paired acquisitions at fit time
risksnone added: gamut projection is unchanged, and an unreliable estimate returns Reinhard exactly

It amplifies existing detail; it does not synthesize any

The added component is exactly Σ(gk − 1)·bk, verified against the implementation at a correlation of 1.0000000000 and a maximum absolute deviation of 8.9e-08. Each band is multiplied by a scalar, so edges keep their position and shape and only local contrast changes. Nothing is invented, which also means nothing genuinely lost to blur is recovered — and that noise in a band is amplified along with its signal. This is why the output is never described as recovered ground-truth detail.

Two rejected variants are worth recording. A version with per-scanner strengths chosen by nested cross-validation improved the image-domain spectrum sevenfold over the locked method but failed a per-fold no-harm gate on S360 and was blocked before any embedding was computed. A slide-adaptive estimator that predicts each slide's gain from its own source energy cut held-out prediction error by 52–57% for AKOYA, GT450 and S60 but did nothing for VERSA and S360 — the two scanners that needed it — and was not adopted.

9 · The target finding

Where you aim decides whether correction helps

The transform, folds, shrinkage, gates and encoding pass are identical across three destinations — AT2, GT450 and S60. The only variable is which scanner the sources are mapped onto, and it inverts the outcome.

The reason is not that AT2 is the softest scanner; by raw transfer it sits mid-panel at 1.000 against GT450's 1.478. What matters is the band power that survives after Reinhard's contrast matching, and on that measure AT2 is near the bottom — GT450 carries 4.14× AT2's fine-band power and S60 2.94×. Aiming at AT2 therefore attenuates 12 of 15 scanner-band combinations; aiming at GT450 or S60 amplifies 12 of 15.

Blurring bought nothing

Aiming at AT2 removed information without purchasing invariance in exchange. Our radius is larger than plain Reinhard's in all four models, and worse than doing nothing at all for UNI v1 and Virchow2. Content non-inferiority breaks for UNI v1 (Δ −0.0338) and Virchow2 (Δ −0.0366) against a −0.02 margin, and the only three collapse failures anywhere in the grid appear there, all in UNI v1, on exactly the two scanners blurred hardest — GT450 variance trace 0.775, S60 0.865, threshold 0.90.

This is section 5's negative result reappearing inside our own method development. Removing high-frequency content lowers image-domain discrepancy while damaging representation, and the pre-registered gates caught it in the models most sensitive to it. It also bounds AT2's role: AT2 is the paired reference because it is the acquisition anchor, not because it is a good destination for harmonization.

The decisive contrast in this report

A linear probe names the scanner at {{PROBE_RAW_MIN}} to {{PROBE_RAW_MAX}} on raw embeddings. After the best image correction available here it is still {{PROBE_IMAGE_BEST}} at worst. After feature-space correction it is {{PROBE_FEATURE_MIN}} to {{PROBE_FEATURE_MAX}} — at or below the chance level of {{PROBE_CHANCE}}. ResNet50's radius falls 32.9% under our image correction while its probe moves only from 0.970 to 0.890; UNI v1 and Virchow2 stay above 0.99 in every image condition and drop to 0.13 and 0.12 under Procrustes.

Radius and probe measure different things. Radius is the geometric spread of six points around their shared centre, and an image correction can pull that in substantially. The probe asks whether any linear direction still separates the scanners, and after image correction one plainly survives. Applying a different linear map per scanner, as CORAL and Procrustes do, moves the distributions onto each other and that direction disappears.

This is the quantitative case for feature-space correction. Measured as drop from raw, the image conditions move the probe by 0.005 to 0.080 while the feature-space affine and rotation move it by 0.807 to 0.920 — two orders of magnitude apart. It is strongest exactly where the image methods are weakest: UNI v1's radius improves 6.0% under Reinhard and 22.5% under Procrustes, and its probe falls 0.006 under our image correction and 0.869 under Procrustes. CORAL is the affine of the pair and the one that needs no paired data to fit, so it is the reference point for a site that cannot rescan.

One anomaly should be recorded rather than smoothed over. CORAL lands below chance, 0.077 to 0.103, consistently across all five folds. A classifier performing worse than chance is systematically wrong, not merely uninformative; the likely cause is that the leave-one-slide-out harmonization gives each slide a slightly different transform, so a direction learned on training slides inverts on held-out ones. Procrustes, which is a rotation and does not rescale, sits much closer to chance. This does not make CORAL better than Procrustes — sub-chance is a measurement warning, not a result — and the shared conclusion holds for both.

The probe is secondary by construction and is never read alone. A constant representation would reach chance while destroying every patch, which is the degeneracy sections 1 and 5 exist to guard against.

12 · Synthesis

Frequency is the bottleneck; feature space is where the room is

The report is one argument in four steps, and the correction in section 7 is the bridge between them rather than the destination.

  1. The scanner effect is spectral, content-dependent and not one-dimensional. Six distinct transfer signatures with intervals excluding zero, a tissue component that ranges from 0.033 to 0.313 and differs by instrument, and a pre-specified scalar blur–sharpen family rejected for all five scanners.
  2. Spatial frequency is the residual once colour is matched, and it is a real lever. Frequency calibration on raw images is worth nothing — +0.5% to −0.5% across four models. The same correction after Reinhard is worth +2.8 to +5.0 points, for three numbers per scanner, no paired data at fit time and no retraining. That the gain is modest but nearly free is the point: it establishes that the residual exists and is correctable, without claiming the correction is the answer.
  3. But the direction matters, and the image domain caps out. Aim at a detail-poor destination and the same estimator destroys content while buying no invariance. Aim well and it helps — and a linear probe still names the scanner at 0.885 or better. That ceiling is not a property of our implementation: a variant with seven times the image-domain gain was blocked by our own no-harm gate, a slide-adaptive estimator helped only the scanners that did not need it, and three destinations were tried.
  4. So the headroom is in feature space. Procrustes reaches +20 to +44% radius reduction where the best image method reaches +4 to +33%, and takes the probe from 0.99 to 0.12. The recommendation this report ends on is therefore not to adopt our correction but to correct where the room is — while noting that the two levels are complementary: image correction is model-independent and produces an artefact a pathologist can inspect, feature correction is fitted per model and produces nothing viewable, and only the second reaches the linear signature.
Not claimed — no clinical or biological preference among scanners is asserted, and nothing here is biological fidelity or clinical validation; the only biological label available is normal tissue type, used as coarse secondary evidence. The multi-target result covers three destinations in one 109-slide cohort; whether the amplification advantage extends past GT450 or to another cohort is untested. The image-level extension is a post-core method development and does not enter the frozen five-method ranking. External validation on an independent multi-scanner resource remains the outstanding gap.

What the field already knows, and what is left

Independent support for where this ends up

ComBat feature harmonization on TCGA colon and stomach cohorts dropped tissue-source-site prediction from AUROC above 0.95 to about 0.506 while microsatellite-instability prediction was unchanged at 0.667 → 0.669. Feature correction erases the technical signature and keeps the biology — with a molecular endpoint attached, which this cohort cannot supply. That is corroboration for the direction section 12 arrives at, from an independent cohort and a different correction.

It is not a competing claim, because feature-space correction is not one of ours. CORAL and orthogonal Procrustes enter this study as comparators taken from the literature, and the conclusion drawn from them is a boundary statement about image space, not a proposal for a new harmonizer. What is novel on this axis is the other half of the contrast: feature correction measured against a fully optimized image correction on the same embeddings and the same slides, which we did not find reported.

Our version is also the sharper one for the scanner question specifically. ComBat removed institution on TCGA, where scanner, staining protocol and site are confounded and cannot be separated; this design scans one physical slide on six instruments, so the label being removed is the scanner and nothing else. The caveat the ComBat authors state transfers directly and we adopt it: batch effects living in feature interactions may survive a linear correction, and a linear probe would not see them.

What the stain-normalization literature measures

This matters because our method sits in that family, so it is worth being precise about what that family reports. The largest benchmark we found — identical tissue stained in 66 laboratories across 11 countries, eight methods including Macenko, Reinhard, Vahadane, CycleGAN and pix2pix — scores methods on colour-distribution agreement in lαβ (histogram intersection, correlation, Euclidean distance, Jensen–Shannon), on FID, and on SSIM. Histogram matching led the colour metrics at 0.891 intersection and 0.938 correlation; Vahadane led SSIM at 0.989, with every method above 0.92.

Two things follow. First, structure is certified by SSIM, and SSIM above 0.92 is compatible with a substantial band-power mismatch: it is a local luminance–contrast–structure product, not a measurement of how much energy sits at each spatial scale. A method can pass it while leaving the sharpness difference this report measures entirely uncorrected. Second, and more directly, that benchmark states it offers limited mechanistic analysis and contains no frequency or spectral decomposition; the deep-learning review likewise covers Fourier-based normalization without discussing amplitude versus phase or whether those methods change high-frequency texture at all.

Where the Fourier transform does appear in this literature, it is used to construct a normalizer or an augmenter. We did not find work that uses it to characterize the difference being corrected — asking, with paired acquisitions of the same tissue, how much of the mismatch lives at each spatial scale and whether colour matching removes it. That inversion is what sections 3 and 9 do, and the same holds on the physics side: whole-slide optical transfer is measured through-focus on custom resolution targets, not on paired tissue across instruments.

The gap has a visible symptom in their own reviews: methods that score better on normalization metrics do not necessarily perform better downstream, and this is named as an open problem. Band power offers one concrete mechanism for it. After Reinhard, GT450 still carries 4.14× AT2's fine-band power and S60 2.94× — a discrepancy no colour-distribution metric can see, and which section 11 shows the models can.

One adjacent result supports our design choice rather than competing with it. The Fourier-domain adaptation rationale holds that convolutional networks are more sensitive to the amplitude spectrum while human recognition leans on phase. Our correction changes band amplitude only and leaves phase untouched by construction, which is why edges keep their position and shape while local contrast changes.

What appears genuinely unclaimed is the pairing of two results: that a pre-registered fidelity constraint changes which corrections are acceptable, and that the destination chosen for harmonization decides whether the same estimator helps or destroys. The stain normalization literature knows reference choice matters, but treats it as picking a representative colour template. The claim here is sharper and physical: what governs the outcome is the target's post-normalization detail power, because that sets whether the correction attenuates or amplifies, and attenuation costs content without buying invariance.

14 · Next

What we would do next, in priority order

External
highest value

PLISM as external mechanistic validation. An independent registered resource of 46 tissue types across 13 stain conditions and 13 devices, with 3,417 aligned image groups, overlapping our panel on several instruments. Band boundaries, transform family, gates and success criteria would be frozen from this cohort before PLISM is opened, and the tiles would not be used to refit anything.

One prerequisite: PLISM tiles are Elastix-registered and resampled to a common reference, so interpolation can alter exactly the high-frequency content we measure. An identity/resampling audit runs first, and if native scans are unavailable the result is reported as effective transfer after PLISM preprocessing, not as scanner transfer.

Downstream
the real gap

A labelled endpoint. Every fidelity measure here is representation-level, because normal tissue type is the only biological label this cohort carries. The ComBat precedent shows the template: pair harmonization with a molecular or clinical readout and report both. Concretely, we would test whether the destination effect survives into a downstream task — does correcting toward a detail-poor target measurably cost subtype or biomarker accuracy, not just cosine geometry?

This is the single change that would move the work from “careful measurement of representation geometry” to “decisions change”.

Generative
built on this, not instead of it

A learned residual stacked on the analytic correction. The band gains are three numbers per scanner; a network could predict a spatially varying residual on top of them, which is exactly the part the analytic form cannot express — the scanner × tissue interaction section 3.2 measures and a scalar gain cannot follow.

The design that keeps the current guarantees is a residual model, not a generator: the analytic output stays the base, the network predicts a correction to it, and the same exact gamut projection closes the pipeline. Then the worst case is still a bounded, inspectable change to structure that is already present, and switching the network off returns exactly what section 7 produces.

It would need its own frozen contract before any endpoint is opened: paired scanner-conditioned training, image-only model selection, an explicit hallucination audit against the paired acquisition, and external validation. The property worth protecting is the one that makes the current method defensible — that it can only rescale structure the scanner actually recorded.

Timing risk. The feature-harmonization lane is active, with at least one paired multi-scanner preprint already combining feature-level correction with PLISM. The characterization, the control-bounded frontier and the destination result are ours regardless, but the feature-space framing is not a lane to enter slowly.
