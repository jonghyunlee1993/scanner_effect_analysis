The lineage

Reinhard, and what we add to it

Reinhard
baseline

Match the per-channel mean and standard deviation of CIE L*a*b* between the source population and the target population. It is the white-balance step: no paired images needed, just a set of source slides and a set of target slides.

It is a real baseline, not a straw man — it is safe and invariance-improving in all four frozen models. What it cannot touch is sharpness. GT450 transmits 1.478× the high-frequency energy of AT2 and AKOYA only 0.335×, and matching colour leaves that untouched.

Ours
RF1U

Same deployment contract as Reinhard — population statistics only, no paired acquisitions anywhere in the fit — with a per-band sharpness correction added.

  1. Split the optical-density mean into three detail bands at σ = 1, 2 and 4 px, keeping the low-pass base that carries colour.
  2. For each band, take the ratio of pooled band energy between the target and source populations. This is Reinhard's moment matching applied per spatial frequency.
  3. Bootstrap the slides to get that ratio's own standard error, and shrink each band toward identity by α = max(0, 1 − 2·SE/|log g|).
  4. Add the resulting correction to all three OD channels as one shared scalar, so hue is preserved.
  5. Project anything out of range onto the exact RGB8 boundary instead of clipping channels independently.

Step 3 is the safety mechanism: a band whose gain cannot be estimated reliably gets α = 0, which is exact identity, and the method degrades to plain Reinhard.

The finding

Why the destination flips the result

The transform, the folds, the shrinkage, the gates and the encoding pass are identical across the three destinations. The only variable is which scanner the sources are mapped onto — and it inverts the outcome.

The reason is not that AT2 is the softest scanner. By raw high-frequency transfer AT2 sits in the middle of the panel: GT450 1.478, S60 1.044, AT2 1.000, VERSA 0.938, S360 0.842, AKOYA 0.335. What matters is the band energy that survives after Reinhard's contrast matching, and on that measure AT2 is near the bottom — GT450 carries 4.14× AT2's fine-band energy and S60 2.94×. Aiming at AT2 therefore means attenuating 12 of 15 scanner-band combinations; aiming at GT450 or S60 means amplifying 12 of 15.

Blurring bought nothing

Aiming at AT2 removed information without purchasing invariance in exchange. Our radius is larger than plain Reinhard's in all four models, and worse than doing nothing at all for UNI v1 and Virchow2. Matching band energy downward produces images that are statistically closer to AT2 and representationally further from it.

The gate failures follow the same line. At the AT2 destination content non-inferiority breaks for UNI v1 (Δ −0.0338) and Virchow2 (Δ −0.0366) against a −0.02 margin, and the only three collapse failures anywhere in the grid appear there too — all in UNI v1, on exactly the two scanners that were blurred hardest (GT450 variance trace 0.775, S60 0.865, threshold 0.90).

Retrieval

What a patch's neighbours actually look like

Scanner radius measures how far apart the six per-scanner centroids sit. It does not say what a retrieval returns. So we asked directly: take one patch, look at its {{NEIGHBOURS}} nearest neighbours among the 600 patches of its own slide, and count how many came from the same scanner it did.

If the representation were perfectly scanner-invariant that share would fall to the chance level of {{CHANCE}}. If the scanner dominates, it stays near one. The two tables move together, which is the whole point. At GT450 and S60 our correction makes neighbours less scanner-bound and more content-accurate at the same time — for ResNet50 aiming at GT450, the same-scanner share falls from 0.549 raw to 0.321 while same-location retrieval climbs from 0.568 to 0.847.

At AT2 both move the wrong way simultaneously. Neighbours become more scanner-bound than under Reinhard alone and the correct physical location is found less often. That is the signature of destroyed content rather than removed nuisance, and it is visible here without reference to any threshold.

Probing

The scanner is still written on the embedding

Radius asks how far the six acquisitions of one location drift from their own centroid. A probe asks the adversarial version: fit a linear classifier on training slides and see whether it can still name the scanner on slides it has never seen. Chance is {{PROBE_CHANCE}}.

Radius falls by a third; the probe barely moves

On raw embeddings a linear probe names the scanner at {{PROBE_RAW_MIN}} to {{PROBE_RAW_MAX}} balanced accuracy. After the best correction available here it is still 0.885 at worst. ResNet50's scanner radius drops 32.9% aiming at GT450 while its probe falls only from 0.970 to 0.890; UNI v1 and Virchow2 sit above 0.99 in every condition, corrected or not.

The two metrics are not in conflict — they measure different things. Radius is the geometric spread of six points around their shared centre, and a correction can pull that spread in substantially. The probe asks whether any linear direction still separates the scanners, and one evidently survives even when the cloud has been compressed. Image-space normalization moves the geometry; it does not delete the signature.

The direction still agrees with everything else: {{PROBE_LOWERED}} of 12 cells lower the probe, and the AT2 destination raises it in all four models. But the magnitudes are a warning about vocabulary. “Scanner invariance” is not one quantity, and a method can improve one measure of it by a third while leaving another essentially untouched.

This endpoint is secondary by construction and is never read alone. A constant representation would drive the probe to chance while destroying every patch, which is the degeneracy the content and collapse gates exist to catch.

Sensitivity and correctability run opposite. UNI is the most scanner-sensitive model at a raw radius of 0.483, yet colour matching removes the least from it, 6.1%. ResNet50 is the reverse: least sensitive at 0.212, and 27.6% of that disappears with colour alone. ResNet50's scanner signal is mostly colour. The pathology models' is not.

The fragile models are the pathology-specific ones. The two that fail content at the AT2 destination are UNI v1 and Virchow2, the self-supervised models trained on fine pathology texture. ResNet50 and CONCH, trained on natural images and on image-caption pairs, absorbed the same blur without breaking. That fits the reading that erasing high frequency erases what the pathology models were trained to encode — but it is a hypothesis from four models, not an established fact.

The direction agrees across all four. Aiming at GT450 or S60 helps every model; aiming at AT2 hurts every model. The magnitudes differ, but no sign flips — which is the strongest evidence that this is a property of the correction and not of one architecture.

Summary

Three sentences

  • What Reinhard leaves behind is sharpness, and matching it band by band helps — but only in the sharpening direction.
  • AT2 is the paired reference, not a good destination. Its post-Reinhard detail energy is near the bottom of the panel, so aiming there means blurring almost everything, and blurring costs content without buying invariance.
  • The pre-registered gates earned their keep. They caught this failure in the models most sensitive to it, and the neighbour composition shows the same thing with no threshold involved at all.
Not claimed here — nothing about clinical or biological preference among scanners. This condition does not enter the frozen five-method ranking and does not replace the locked method. Three destinations in one 109-slide cohort; whether the amplification advantage extends past GT450, or to another cohort, is untested.
